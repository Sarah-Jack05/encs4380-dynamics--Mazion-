First-Order Time Response

$$\tau \dot{y} + y = Kx$$ $$G(s) = \frac{K}{\tau s + 1}$$
  • $x$: input (the tea temperature)
  • $y$: output (the thermometer reading)
  • $K$: steady-state gain (how much $y$ ends up per unit of $x$)
  • $\tau$: time constant (time to reach 63.2% of the final change)

Module 2: First-Order Frequency Response

Predict, then check: if τ gets bigger and the input frequency stays the same, what happens to the amplitude and to the phase lag?

Check

The amplitude gets smaller and the phase lag gets bigger. Both formulas use ωτ, and a bigger τ makes ωτ bigger. Example: τ = 0.2 s and ω = 2 rad/s give an amplitude ratio of 0.93 and a lag of 21.8°.
